Is AI Helpful Or Making Us Too Dependent ?

A closer look at how artificial intelligence is changing the way we think, learn and work.

By Dakshaya Chaudhary · 24 July 2026

The world of Artificial Intelligence also known as AI is vast. It is a contributor to reshaping our daily lives. These days AI has been growing like bamboo. A US Fed shows that the higher the investment in AI means the higher the growth of GDP. This means that the AI isn’t just anything it is a powerful asset to have within your mind. Recent studies often show that usage of AI is increasing day-by-day. People love to ask irrelevant questions the most like “What happens if I don’t brush for 3 days”. This is something most of us will find in our daily behaviour.


Research Articles



A study shows that 83% of students use ChatGPT for writing essays and shortly afterward they are unable to recall, even in rare cases students are sitting blank during an exam. This same effect is noticeable with experts that in a workplace they often are unable to propose ideas or either they are relying too much on AI which could lead to “significant skill collapse”. Other concerns which arose are that people will get their jobs replaced. In the point of concerns there are many things like Job replacement, Better Intelligence than humans, Privacy risks, Deepfakes, Misinformation and more are some of the common concerns.

Human Perspective



Farhan, Grade 8: While interviewing his responses were straight forward. He said that he uses AI for technology and it make his day easier and is very helpful he uses the AI for almost all tasks and addresses the accessibility.
Vargavi, Grade 8: She address that “AI is useful when you use it wisely for homework and learning, and the vastness of the intelligence and speedy quick responses is what makes a difference between a human brain and a artificial brain.” She expressed concerns regarding the privacy and replacement of Jobs.
Teacher: “I use AI to simplify my work like making quizzes and all. In terms of dependency the rates are high as it could take over the human brain and make children too much dependable on AI even for simple tasks. It cannot replace the jobs of a teacher as it cannot bring that emotions to learning.”
Reddit User: “Absolutely. When ChatGPT had an outage recently, I saw people literally posting on LinkedIn that they didn’t know what to do because they had outsourced their thinking to it. It’s just profoundly stupid. AI tools have their place and are a god-send when used appropriately, but any reliance on them is going to lead to significant problems with people who forget basic things like how to make decisions or complete an original thought.”

Analysis


A simple thing that can be retrieved from all the interviews is that people find AI not as a machine but more like a mentor for almost everything. People are often afraid of AI raiding their jobs but it is quite understandable that AI is still learning from the millions of data it has been feeded. In terms of dependency, humans are always lazy to do work on their on own they always want somebody for doing their tasks which is exactly same in the case of AI usage. Parents are afraid of their children using calculator but not using AI. AI is everywhere even from our morning routines to our night routines either it is about whipping a cream or just relaxing for 10 minutes. Studies show this more deeply people want to make their life easier and efficient. AI has helped many people in improving but also has made cognitive skills get weaker over time. AI’s dependency is something that is indeed a trap for human brain. Skills like analytical skills, problem solving and many more capabilities of our brain are the main prey of the AI dependency. One thing that is indeed a point to acknowledge is basically the accessibility of AI it just takes some clicks to open the AI and ask a question.

Conclusion


AI has undoubtedly become a powerful part of modern life. From simplifying everyday tasks to helping students learn and professionals work more efficiently, its usefulness is difficult to ignore. However, the interviews and research also raise an important concern: convenience can gradually become dependence. The problem may not be AI itself, but how we choose to use it. When AI supports human thinking, it can be an incredibly useful tool. When it begins replacing our ability to think, create, decide, or solve problems independently, its usefulness can become a weakness. Perhaps the real question is not whether AI is helping us or making us dependent, but whether we are still the ones controlling the tool.
