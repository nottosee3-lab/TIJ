Is AI Helpful or Making Us Too Dependent?

Artificial intelligence can explain difficult concepts, speed up everyday tasks, and make information easier to access. But when does useful assistance become dependence—and what happens to our ability to think independently?

By Dakshaya Chaudhary 24 July 2026 Technology & Education

Artificial intelligence has become a familiar part of everyday life. Students use AI tools to understand lessons, people use them to organise ideas, and businesses explore ways to complete tasks more efficiently. What once seemed futuristic is now available through a phone or computer.

However, the growing convenience raises an important question: are we using AI to improve our abilities, or are we gradually allowing it to do too much of our thinking for us?

The convenience of artificial intelligence

One of AI's biggest advantages is accessibility. A student who struggles with a difficult topic can ask for a simpler explanation, request examples, or practise questions. Someone learning a new skill can receive feedback and explore ideas without always needing immediate access to an expert.

AI can also help people organise information, check their work, brainstorm possibilities, and save time on repetitive tasks. Used thoughtfully, it can support learning rather than replace it.

Yet convenience can change behaviour. If a person immediately asks AI to solve every difficult problem, they may miss the opportunity to struggle productively, make mistakes, and develop their own reasoning.

Are students becoming too dependent?

Education is one of the clearest places to examine this concern. AI can explain a mathematical method or help a student understand a scientific concept. But there is a difference between learning how to solve a problem and copying a solution that someone—or something—has already produced.

For example, a student who asks AI to explain each step of a maths problem and then solves a similar problem independently is using the tool as a learning aid. A student who copies the final answer without understanding it may finish the assignment but gain little knowledge.

Claims about the percentage of students using AI for essays or homework should be treated carefully. Results can differ according to the country, age group, survey date, and wording of the questions. Without a verifiable original study, a specific percentage should not be presented as a confirmed fact.

The important question is not simply whether students use AI, but whether they can still explain and apply what they submit.

What about jobs and human skills?

AI is also changing how people approach work. Some tasks that once required substantial time can now be completed more quickly with automated tools. This may improve productivity, but it can also create uncertainty about which skills employers will value and how certain jobs may change.

It would be too simplistic to conclude that AI will eliminate every job or that people will inevitably lose their abilities. The effects depend on the occupation, the technology being used, how organisations adopt it, and whether workers receive opportunities to develop new skills.

Economic research on AI investment and productivity should also be read precisely. Investment in AI may contribute to economic growth, but that relationship alone does not prove that AI investment automatically causes higher GDP growth in every situation.

What people think about AI

Discussions about AI often reveal different priorities. Students may value its ability to explain difficult topics and reduce the time needed for assignments. Teachers may be concerned about whether submitted work reflects a student's own understanding. People discussing AI online may describe useful experiences, frustrating mistakes, or worries about future changes.

These perspectives can help identify questions worth investigating, but individual experiences are not proof of what all students, teachers, or workers believe. Interviews and online comments should be represented accurately, with permission where appropriate, and distinguished from findings based on larger studies.

The risks we should not ignore

1. We may stop checking our own reasoning

AI-generated answers can sound confident even when they contain mistakes. Accepting an answer without checking it can allow errors to spread and weaken a person's habit of questioning information.

2. Privacy matters

Users may enter personal details, school information, private messages, or other sensitive material into AI tools without considering how that information is handled. People should read the relevant privacy settings and avoid sharing information they do not need to disclose.

3. Misinformation can look convincing

AI can produce inaccurate text and realistic-looking images, audio, or other media. A polished presentation does not guarantee that something is true. Checking the original source and comparing reliable reports remain essential habits.

4. Independent skills need practice

Writing, problem-solving, research, and critical thinking improve through practice. If AI consistently performs these tasks before a person attempts them, that person may have fewer opportunities to build those abilities. The risk is not inevitable, but it is worth taking seriously.

Using AI without surrendering our judgement

The answer does not have to be a complete rejection of AI. Instead, people can use it in ways that keep their own thinking active.

Students can try a question independently before asking for help. They can request hints rather than finished answers, verify important claims using reliable sources, and explain the final answer in their own words. Teachers and schools can also make expectations about AI use clearer.

In workplaces, AI-generated material should be checked by people who understand the task. Decisions with significant consequences need appropriate human judgement, accountability, and review.

Conclusion: a tool, not a replacement for thinking

Artificial intelligence offers real benefits. It can make information more accessible, support learning, and help people complete certain tasks efficiently. At the same time, careless dependence can encourage people to accept answers without understanding them or verifying whether they are true.

The distinction lies in how the tool is used. AI can help us think through a problem, but it should not become a reason to stop thinking about it. Convenience is valuable when it strengthens our abilities—not when it replaces the effort needed to develop them.

Editorial note: This article discusses general concerns and possible benefits of AI. Specific statistics, economic findings, and claims about AI's effects should be checked against original, reliable sources before being treated as established evidence.
