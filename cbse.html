CBSE Insights: Understanding the 3R Rule

By Dakshaya Chaudhary 1 August 2026 CBSE & Education

On April 2, 2026 CBSE released a implementation plan. This rule stated that from the beginning of the 2026-27 academic session there will be a new language rule or 3R rule. This rule was compiled in Nation Curriculum Framework for School Education (NCF-SE). This rule will begin from class 6 students who are in 2026-27 session. This was a just a implementation plan till May 15, 2026. On this day it declared that now onwards the 3R rule will be followed, under Circular No. Acad-33/2026. But it became a rule nationwide on July 1, 2026.

About 3R's

The 3R simply mean R1, R2 and R3, this law states that any student must have R2 and R3 as the native Indian Language (Bhartiya Bhasha). And the R1 can be a foreign language or can be also chosen as a native. Though, this isn’t it there can be a R4 as well depending upon the school and R4 will not be a credited subject. This rule further states that the native languages must be distinct not like Hindi core and Hindi elective.

The Examination System

Class 10:

There will be no change in the aspiring board examiners and the 3R rule will not be sanctioned on them.

Class 7, 8 & 9:

There will be a change for these students and they will have to give a exam for R3 but the twist is that it will be done 100% internally.

Class 6 & below:

There will be a full change and the students who are in following criteria must give a board exam for all the languages.

The Challenge

The students who are in Class 9 are having a challenge of textbook as the plan became a rule on July 1, 2026. NCERT publications were not able to form a whole new textbook for Class 9 students. Due to this students are now continuing with the Class 6 R3 textbooks. This has been a clear failure at the end of the education system but the rule was also delayed in publishing.

Conclusion

A final decision whether it is good or bad is perhaps. Many journalists are giving this a political angle though there is no clarification in the political angle. But this is an admired decision by our system as now our cultural identity is preserved with this decision. This decision may or may not change our education system but it is a step towards securing our heritage. In the end, the conclusion stands that it isn’t sure about the recognition of this rule but still the question arises “Is this a step towards Politics? ”.
